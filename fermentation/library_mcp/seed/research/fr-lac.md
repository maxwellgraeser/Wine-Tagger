# fr-lac: Loire, Alsace, Champagne, national-level France — research notes

## Sources

- INAO (inao.gouv.fr) — per-appellation "fiche produit" pages, used as the
  primary register for every AOC in Pays Nantais, Anjou-Saumur, Touraine,
  Centre-Loire, Auvergne/upper Loire, Alsace, Champagne and Lorraine.
  Accessed 2026-09.
- eAmbrosia (ec.europa.eu/agriculture/eambrosia) corroborates PDO/PGI
  status for the same list; INAO's own fiches were used as primary since
  they carry the cahier des charges directly.
- `divineloire.fr` (Loire wine board) — sub-denomination lists for Anjou
  and Touraine not broken out on INAO's summary fiches (Anjou Mousseux,
  Anjou Coteaux de la Loire, Anjou Gamay; Touraine's five named
  geographical denominations).
- `muscadet.fr` — the 7 Muscadet Sèvre et Maine crus communaux.
- `fiefsvendeens.com` — the 5 Fiefs Vendéens named zones.
- `vins-centre-loire.com` — confirms "Centre-Loire" as the trade name for
  the Sancerre/Pouilly/Menetou-Salon/Quincy/Reuilly group.
- `vinsalsace.com` (CIVA) — Alsace AOC and Alsace Grand Cru structure.
- `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace_Grand_Cru_AOC` — the current list of 51
  named Grand Cru AOCs (cross-checked against CIVA's own page, which
  lists the crus without a single machine-readable table).
- `champagne.fr` (Comité Champagne) — the 5 informal sub-areas of
  Champagne.
- `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_Champagne_vineyards` — the 17
  Grand Cru villages and their sub-area placement; INAO does not publish
  the 100%-classification village list itself (that scale predates and
  sits outside the AOC cahier des charges).
- `igpvaldeloire.fr` cahier des charges PDF (2021) — Val de Loire IGP.
- Wikipedia used only as a fallback for facts INAO/CIVA/interprofession
  sites don't tabulate (e.g. individual cru names, Moselle AOC, Côtes de
  Toul), never as the sole source for an appellation's existence.

## Changes to existing entries

- **`IGP Val de Loire` → `Val de Loire`** (per the reviewer's explicit
  instruction, `was: "IGP Val de Loire"` added). "IGP" is a classification
  word the lookup already strips, so the bare form is the correct name.
- **Dropped `"Val de Loire"` from `Loire Valley`'s synonym list.** Once the
  IGP entry is named exactly "Val de Loire", the name would collide with
  Loire Valley's existing synonym of the same string (checker: "duplicate
  label 'Val de Loire' in FR"). Loire Valley is our scope's national
  entry, so `Loire Valley` is included in this file with that one synonym
  removed; `Loire`, `Vallée de la Loire` and `Vallee de la Loire` are kept.
  This is a real ambiguity in French usage — "Val de Loire" is used both
  for the whole valley and for the IGP — but a name may not equal another
  entry's synonym in the same country, and the IGP is the more specific,
  more label-relevant sense (it appears on millions of bottles; "Val de
  Loire" as a bare synonym for the whole valley is rarer on labels than
  "Vallée de la Loire").
- **`Anjou-Villages Brissac` renamed to `Anjou Brissac`** (`was:` added),
  matching the appellation's current official name; old form kept as a
  synonym.
- **`Alsace`'s existing entry had `Alsace Grand Cru` and `Crémant
  d'Alsace` incorrectly listed as its synonyms.** Both are their own AOCs
  with their own cahiers des charges and grape rules, not alternate names
  for the base Alsace AOC. Split them out as separate entries, each with
  `parent: "Alsace"`.
- **`Centre-Loire` re-parented to `Loire Valley` as an informal node**
  (no `classification`) rather than leaving Sancerre/Pouilly-Fumé/etc.
  parented directly to bare `Loire Valley`. This is the nesting a
  retailer or sommelier would use ("Centre-Loire" is the standard trade
  term for this group), and keeps one parent per entry consistent with
  the other Loire sub-groups (Pays Nantais's members already parent to
  `Loire Valley` directly since there is no equivalent umbrella term in
  trade use for that group).
- No other existing FR entries in this scope were found in
  `regions.yaml` beyond the ones listed above and the base `Champagne`
  entry, which is unchanged in content (grapes/classification confirmed
  against INAO, no rename).

## Homonyms

- **`Moselle`** (Lorraine AOC, FR) shares its name with the German
  **Mosel** wine region — flagged per the scope note. Kept as separate
  entries in different countries; no synonym overlap since the German
  region is spelled "Mosel" (or "Moselle" in French/English usage for the
  German river/region too, which is a real risk — a retailer page saying
  "Moselle" could mean either country's wine. Left as-is since both
  countries' entries are legitimate and the tagger resolves by whichever
  country context accompanies the wine).
- **`La Rioja`** — not in this scope (fr-lac has no Rioja-named entry);
  noted here only because the brief calls out this pattern as an example,
  not because it recurs in this file.
- No other cross-country name collisions were found for entries in this
  file.

## Uncertain calls

- **Klevener de Heiligenstein as a synonym of "Savagnin Rose".** VIVC and
  most Alsace sources treat Klevener de Heiligenstein as the same variety
  as Savagnin Rose (a pink-berried Traminer relative, distinct from
  Gewürztraminer/Savagnin Blanc). Flagging because "Traminer" nomenclature
  in this family is notoriously tangled; if a future reviewer has VIVC
  access and disagrees, this pairing should be split.
- **Sauvignon Gris = Fié Gris.** Treated as one variety (a grey-berried
  Sauvignon Blanc mutation, VIVC "Sauvignon Gris"), used under the local
  Loire name Fié Gris. Reasonably confident, flagging only because it is
  a color mutation rather than a fully distinct variety and some sources
  discuss it loosely.
- **Grolleau Gris as its own entry rather than a synonym of `Grolleau`.**
  `Grolleau` (red, already in `grapes.yaml`) and Grolleau Gris are
  distinct color mutations grown and labeled separately in Anjou; kept
  as a separate grape entry rather than merged, matching the "Malvasia
  di Schierano is not Malvasia di Casorzo" caution in the brief.
- **Coteaux du Layon village synonyms**: some retailer pages hyphenate
  ("Coteaux-du-Layon-Chaume") and some don't; both forms should resolve,
  but only the space-separated forms already used by INAO/divineloire
  were added as synonyms to avoid guessing at permutations not seen in
  the wild.
- **Alsace Grand Cru grape lists**: CIVA's own page states all 51 crus
  permit Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris and Muscat except Zotzenberg
  (Sylvaner only) and Kaefferkopf (a blend rule, listed here with the
  4 varieties as its principal grapes per its cahier, though Kaefferkopf
  is technically a mandatory-blend AOC rather than a varietal-choice one
  like the others — flagging in case a reviewer wants Kaefferkopf's
  `grapes:` field handled differently).

## Remaining checker warnings

- `Loire Valley: no source` — unchanged carry-forward of the pre-existing
  library entry, which itself has no `source:` field.
- `Quarts de Chaume: 'Quarts de Chaume Grand Cru' has a classification
  word or (…) tail` and `Alsace Grand Cru: 'Alsace Grand Cru' has a
  classification word or (…) tail` — both are real label forms ("Quarts
  de Chaume Grand Cru" is the appellation's full current name; "Alsace
  Grand Cru" is the parent AOC's own name, which necessarily contains
  "Grand Cru"). The lookup already strips a trailing/leading "Grand Cru",
  so these are harmless; kept as-is rather than removed, since dropping
  them would lose real label text.
- `Val de Loire: 'Vin de Pays du Val de Loire' has a classification word`
  and `Vin de France: 'France (Vin de France)' has a classification
  word` — same pattern: the former official name and a parenthetical
  retailer form, both harmless once the classification words are
  stripped.
- `Pinot Gris: synonym 'Tokay' is also a region: Tokaj (HU)` — "Tokay" was
  the historic Alsace name for Pinot Gris (now legally restricted, but
  still seen on older labels and secondary-market listings) and
  coincidentally the old English spelling of Hungary's Tokaj. Both uses
  are real; left as a documented collision rather than removed, per the
  brief's allowance for cross-country homonyms.

## Review (main session, 2026-09-26)

- `Pinot Gris`: dropped the bare synonym `Tokay`. On a modern page it
  means Tokaj (HU) or Australian Tokay (Muscadelle), the ambiguous-bare-name
  and place-name patterns. `Tokay d'Alsace` and `Tokay Pinot Gris` stay.
- `Zotzenberg`: `grapes:` is now Sylvaner, Riesling, Gewürztraminer and
  Pinot Gris. The cahier adds Sylvaner to the usual Grand Cru varieties
  (Muscat excluded); it doesn't restrict the cru to Sylvaner.
- Accepted: `IGP Val de Loire` → `Val de Loire`, with "Val de Loire" dropped
  from `Loire Valley`; Alsace Grand Cru and Crémant d'Alsace split out of
  Alsace's synonyms; Anjou Brissac rename; Centre-Loire informal node;
  Klevener de Heiligenstein = Savagnin Rose; Sauvignon Gris = Fié Gris;
  Grolleau Gris separate; Kaefferkopf's four grapes.
- Remaining warnings accepted: "Vin de Pays du Val de Loire" (the strip
  leaves "du Val de Loire", so the synonym is needed), Quarts de Chaume
  Grand Cru and Alsace Grand Cru (official names), and "France (Vin de
  France)".
- Merge (2026-09-26): `Tressallier` folded into fr-bjs's `Sacy` as a
  synonym (the same variety per VIVC; the Allier name). `Orbois` (Menu
  Pineau) dropped: no Wikidata item, so it would block the build.
