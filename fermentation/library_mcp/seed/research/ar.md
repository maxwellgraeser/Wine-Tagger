## Counts (running)
- Official register: 101 IG entries + 2 DOC entries (same 2 places also appear as IG rows) = 99 distinct denominations.
- Scope cap: ~80. Producing well under that (~60), because the scope names a specific curated set of departments/districts (export-label priority) rather than every administrative department-level IG. See "Left out" below.

## Left out of the official register (with reason)
Department/locality-level IGs that are on the register but not on the scope's list and not seen on export labels, skipped for the ~80 cap and scope's "priority: export labels" instruction:
- San Juan: 25 de Mayo, 9 de Julio, Albardón, Angaco, Caucete, Chimbas, Iglesia, Jáchal, Pocito, Rawson, Rivadavia (SJ), San Martín (SJ), Sarmiento, Santa Lucía, Barreal, Pozo de los Algarrobos, Valle Fértil, Valle del Pedernal's parent department Sarmiento is folded into Pedernal Valley itself.
- Mendoza: Godoy Cruz, Guaymallén, Junín, La Paz, Las Heras, Lavalle/Desierto de Lavalle, Rivadavia (Mza), San Martín (Mza), Santa Rosa, Distrito Medrano, El Paraíso (both are minor Maipú-area IGs, superseded on labels by Lunlunta/Barrancas/Russel).
- La Rioja: Arauco, Castro Barros, Felipe Varela, General Lamadrid, Sanagasta, San Blas de los Sauces, Vinchina, Valle de Chañarmuyo, Valles del Famatina.
- Neuquén: Confluencia.
- Río Negro: Avellaneda, General Conesa, General Roca, Pichimahuida.
- Catamarca: Belén, Pomán, Santa María, Tinogasta (province-level Catamarca entry only).
- Córdoba: Colón, Colonia Caroya, Córdoba Argentina (kept as the "Córdoba" province entry itself), Cruz del Eje, San Javier.
- Buenos Aires: Villa Ventana.
- Jujuy: none skipped besides not adding a synonym set; Quebrada de Humahuaca is included (real export label use).
- San Luis (its own province with one IG "San Luis") and "Cuyo" (interprovincial IG covering Mendoza+San Juan+La Rioja) are not in the scope's province list; skipped entirely.
These are all real, valid IGs; they were left out only because the scope's explicit list didn't name them and they cap volume/production is dominated by the departments named in the scope.

## Changes to existing entries
- `Luján de Cuyo`: added `classification: DOC`; removed synonyms "Agrelo", "Perdriel", "Vistalba" (each is now its own child entry per the brief's "sub-appellation is a child, not a synonym" rule); added `grapes`.
- `San Rafael`: added `classification: DOC` and `grapes`.
- `Uco Valley`: removed synonyms "Gualtallary", "Paraje Altamira", "La Consulta", "Vista Flores" — each promoted to its own entry nested under the correct department (Tupungato, San Carlos or Tunuyán), since a sub-appellation must be a child entry, never a synonym of a grandparent.
- `San Juan`: removed synonym "Pedernal Valley" — promoted to its own entry `Pedernal Valley` (parent San Juan; the official IG is a specific zone in Sarmiento department, not a synonym for the whole province).
- `Cafayate`: re-parented from `Salta` to `Calchaquí Valley` (nearest enclosing viticultural unit; the current parent is the whole province, but the trade nests Cafayate inside the Calchaquí Valley).

## Renames / naming for homonyms within AR
- `San Carlos` exists as a department in both Mendoza (Uco Valley) and Salta (Calchaquí Valley). Named them `San Carlos, Mendoza` and `San Carlos, Salta` (comma-qualified canonical name), following the existing library convention for same-name disambiguation within one country (e.g. `California Shenandoah Valley`, `Red Hill Douglas County, Oregon`). Comma-separated forms are already stripped/handled by the tagger per the brief, so this does not create spurious literal-match requirements.

## Homonyms with other countries
- `La Rioja` (AR province) vs `La Rioja` (Spain DOCa) — both are legitimate, unrelated.
- `Patagonia` (AR, multi-province umbrella) vs `Patagonia` (CL) — cl.regions.yaml owns the Chilean entry; this file's `Patagonia` is the Argentine homonym only.
- `San Carlos` is also a very common place name elsewhere (Uruguay, Chile, Philippines, etc.) besides the two AR instances above.

## Uncertain calls
- `Cafayate`: Disposición INV Nº 08/2005 says only "Valle de Cafayate" may be used as the registered IG on labels, because "Cafayate" itself is a trademark — but nearly all retail copy still says plain "Cafayate" as the place name. Kept the existing library name `Cafayate` (per "keep existing names unless wrong") and added "Valle de Cafayate" as a synonym.
- `Zonda`: the register has both a department-level `Zonda` IG and a narrower `Valle de Zonda` IG (Resolución C.04/2015) inside the same department. Treated as one entry `Zonda` with `Valle de Zonda` as a synonym rather than two nested entries, since retail copy does not distinguish them; flagging this as a simplification.
- `Perdriel`, `Vistalba`, `Ugarteche`, `Mayor Drummond`, `Cruz de Piedra`, `Gualtallary`, `Médanos`, `San Patricio del Chañar`, `East Mendoza`, `Payogasta`: none of these are on the official INV IG register (checked against the full register text). They are added anyway per the scope's explicit instruction and because they are common export-label/trade names; classification left blank (informal) for all of them.
- `Torrontés Sanjuanino` and `Torrontés Mendocino`: kept as new grapes distinct from canonical `Torrontés` (= Riojano), per VIVC and ampelographic sources treating all three as separate crosses. Flagging in case the merge step wants closer identity review.
